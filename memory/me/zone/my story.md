# a long strange trip

Many decades since my first gig as a programmer, after working for tiny startups and gigantic corporations, I have not lost my love of programming. My artistic soul is nourished by the exploration of 'active art' — beautiful tools that do things that are unpleasant, tedious or difficult to remember how. Tools the fit the human mind like an age-softened baseball mitt. I am having fun facing challenges that push my own limits, discovering new capabilities.

Now, with AI, those limits have shrunken. Duh. As an example, twenty years ago, it took me nearly three years to write my first big solo app, a 3D design tool. With AI, it took two months to make it better and faster. How can that not be exciting?

As with all dreams of success, the reality of using AI beyond those first two months brought me back to earth. Turns out that AI has weaknesses that slow me down, confuse me, forcing me to think differently — no longer about designing software — the strategies for managing AI. And as we all know, thinking is hard. AI cannot do this kind of thinking, making me the bottleneck.

I've always been the bottleneck, always struggling when the answers were located 'outside the box' of my experiences and understanding. So, yeah, this is solid ground for me. I had a temporary but fragile dream of boundless creativity, and woke up.

Okay, so now what?
