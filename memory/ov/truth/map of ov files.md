# Map of ov files

Overview's files. Update this when files are added, moved, or removed.

## root config

- [vite.config.ts](../../../vite.config.ts) — the dev server (port 5185, read from the hub's ports file) and the build. It also lets the server reach outside this folder, which is what makes the guides in the other collections readable at all.
- [package.json](../../../package.json) — two dependencies only: the color math and the markdown reader.
- [svelte.config.js](../../../svelte.config.js), [tsconfig.json](../../../tsconfig.json) — the compiler and type-check settings.
- [vitest.config.ts](../../../vitest.config.ts) — the test runner: anything ending in `.test.ts` under the source folder.
- [index.html](../../../index.html) — the page the app mounts into.
- [CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md) — the project entry point.

## src/lib/svelte/main/ — the frame

- [App.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/App.svelte) — the root. Pushes the four theme colors onto the page whenever any of them changes, starts the one hover-hint watcher, and works out the layout from the real window width: details column and content box side by side, or — when the window is too narrow for both — details alone. Shows nothing but "setting up the overview browser..." until the guides are read. Reads the latest build number off the build-notes table and holds the backdrop that shows them.
- [Controls.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/Controls.svelte) — the row across the top, on the accent: the hamburger that shows or hides details, then the build-number button, then a spacer that takes up the rest so those two stay together at the left.
- [Details.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/Details.svelte) — the column at the left, holding two things that fold: preferences and repair. Which are open is saved as a list of their names; all shut saves an empty list, and nothing saved at all means all open. The page color runs from its first line down to its last, with the accent above and below, and a closing line is drawn under the last section only while it stands open.
- [Operation.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/Operation.svelte) — the content box beside details. It shows exactly one of two things: reading a guide, or looking through them. A guide the list no longer shows closes itself rather than showing nothing.
- [Browse.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/Browse.svelte) — looking through the guides: the filters, the count row, and the list, stacked flush so each holds its own gap and nothing adds a second helping. The count row carries a show/hide folders button at the far left, an unsorted button beside it, and the count pinned to the middle of the whole row. The folders button hides when the filters leave nothing; the unsorted button shows only when the folders are hidden, a sort is on, and more than one file is left.
- [Editor.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/main/Editor.svelte) — the frame around one guide: the top row, and the three parts stacked under it. The row carries the step marks, the folders above the file, the file's name pinned to the middle of the whole width, and the three buttons at the far right — hand it to Obsidian, send it in a message, throw it away. A press anywhere in that block that isn't on one of its controls goes back to the list, and the block lights while the cursor is over an empty part of it; the Escape key does the same. Holding a step mark down keeps stepping, and the arrow keys do the same. Two things are shared rather than owned by any one part, so they live here: the whole file's text, which both the labels and a piece being changed write to, and the short line along the bottom that says what a dead link or a refused write has to say.

## src/lib/svelte/content/ — what the content box shows

- [Files_List.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/Files_List.svelte) — the list itself: every folder and file, folders leading their contents, each row indented by how deep it sits. A row's whole width answers to a click — a file opens for reading, a folder opens or shuts — and carries the hover hint; the little triangle keeps its own click and stops it from reaching the row. The column titles are buttons while the folders are hidden: clicking one walks it through smallest first, largest first, and not sorting, and more than one column can sort at once, with a small number saying where each comes in the order. A project column appears between kind and name, but only while the folders are hidden and no project is picked. The header sits still above the scrolled rows and stands only as tall as its line: the line lies along its top edge and the titles are pulled out of the flow to ride it, taking no height, so whatever stands above measures its gap to the line and the rows stand clear below. The header as a whole stands in front of the line, since lifting it makes a world of its own that nothing inside can reach out of. The list remembers where it was scrolled. The line between rows starts well in from the left, painted under the first column rather than drawn as its edge. Whether there is actually a scrollbar is measured rather than assumed, since a stylesheet cannot ask.
- [Markdown_Editor.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/Markdown_Editor.svelte) — one guide's own words. Its text is read here, turned from markdown into a page, held only while it is on screen, and let go on close. A click on any piece opens it in a box holding the file's own words for those lines; leaving the box writes just those lines back. Each heading is named after its own words so a link ending in a heading has something to land on. A click on a link follows it — down the page, to another guide, or out to a new tab — and a link that leads nowhere says why. Every link is registered with the hits manager as it is drawn, so each answers for its own place and its own words; which one was pressed is still worked out from the press. The words are styled here — paragraphs stepped in and spaced, the six heading levels colored, a line under the top heading — and punctuation is left exactly as the file writes it, so a piece reads the same on the page as in the box that edits it. Every heading below the first carries a soft pointer in the left margin that folds its section away, and the top heading's folds them all; the left inset sits inside the scrolled box so those pointers are not clipped away. The bar beside the words carries a second marker showing where the browser alone would have put its thumb. Where a guide was left is remembered as the scrolling settles — the line the topmost piece began on, never a distance — so coming back to it comes back to the same words; the title stays at the box top, so it is passed over when that line is read.
- [Back_Links.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/Back_Links.svelte) — which guides point at the one being read, each a pill that opens it. A section of its own below the words, drawn only where something points here — an empty one reads as something still being worked out and takes space from the words above it. The gathering is the manager's; this only shows what it worked out.
- [D_Preferences.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/D_Preferences.svelte) — the accent color picker. **ji**
- [D_Repair.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/D_Repair.svelte) — two buttons in the details column for putting right what nothing on screen would show as wrong: mending the index files, since a move can only mend the two folders it touched, and looking through every guide for links that lead nowhere. Each says in the log that it was asked for; the work itself is the files manager's.
- [Report.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/content/Report.svelte) — words too many for the one line along the bottom, read in the whole content box with space to scroll, and a cross at the top left that takes them away. It says when it was made, since a report is kept across a reload and a week-old one otherwise reads like this minute's. A dead link listed in it opens its guide with the link's own drawn words looked for and lit — its address is never looked for, since only the words are ever on the page — and the back mark then comes here rather than to another file.

## src/lib/svelte/filter/ — narrowing what is shown, and looking inside it

- [Browse_Filters.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/filter/Browse_Filters.svelte) — the four filters across the top: words looked for in titles and descriptions, one project at a time, one kind at a time, and any number of tags. The kinds row also offers `none`, which leaves only the files carrying no labels at all. Each has its own bar with its own word above it. The search field takes the whole top row, and the heavy line under it carries one word that folds every picking row at a press — reading just `filters` while they show, and what each holds while they don't. Each row also folds behind its own word. Every fold is remembered. What kinds and tags exist isn't known until the files are read, so both lists fill themselves the moment that finishes.
- [Editor_Filters.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/filter/Editor_Filters.svelte) — what one guide is labeled: its title, its date, the line saying what it is for, its one kind, and its tags. Nothing is typed as free text where it matters — the kind and the tags are picked from the only lists the app accepts. The line above folds the whole form away and then says what the file is labeled; the kinds and the tags each fold behind their own word too. Any change writes the labels back to the file at once and tells the list, so what is on screen agrees with the file without every file being read again.
- [Search.svelte](../../../src/lib/svelte/filter/Search.svelte) — looking through the guide on screen: the search row, and the one place highlighted in the words. The count and two step marks appear beside the field once there is something to look for, and walk the places found, wrapping at both ends. The words looked for are the very ones typed into the list's search, so they carry between the two screens and across a reload. A place inside a folded section is shown while it is highlighted; the piece already open is left alone when the next place is inside it, since folding and unfolding the same piece is what blinked on every keystroke.

## src/lib/ts/ — logic

- [main.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/main.ts) — the entry point. Pushes the stacking layers, the sizes and the fixed inks onto the page, reads three of them back off the page to prove the bridge works, then reads every guide once before letting the app show itself.
- [common/Core.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/common/Core.ts) — everything ov adopts from core, one line each, reached through the "core" alias that tsconfig, vite.config and vitest.config all name. Through it come the one source for every size (`k`), the call that mirrors those numbers onto the page, the color math and the four theme colors, the additions to text and number handling, the diagnostic log, and — adopted 31 August 2026 — a title riding a separator, a press with what was held down alongside it, the sections of the details column, what a thing under the cursor is, the shared types, how much of a run of words fits, a number written in thousands, which pieces a heading owns, a box growing to its natural height, where a scrollbar's thumb would have been drawn, angles and their directions, points and rectangles, the whole hits manager and the element that registers with it, the timers behind a long press and a held repeat, the drawn shapes, the arithmetic behind a section and the spacing of the lines across a view, the rules behind the step marks, the hover hint's plumbing, and six components — a section, a separator, the steppers, the hint itself, the pill that holds one area of tags, the line of words along the bottom, the build-notes popup — which is handed its table by App, since core names no file inside a host — and the stack of sections. The global stylesheet comes from core too, imported by main.ts as `core/main.css`, and one section of a stack is core's type. Nothing in ov is a second copy of anything in core. Only this file reaches through the alias; every other ov file imports from here, components included. **⟵core**
- [managers/Files.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Files.ts) — where the guides come from. The dispatcher is asked what markdown files sit under every collection's guides and designs folders, and that list is the whole picture — nothing is settled when the app's code is prepared, so a file added, moved or thrown away shows straight away. Each file is read once, its five labels kept, its text let go — the reading of those labels is the label utility's. Index files are left out entirely. A file arriving with no labels gets a block composed from its own words and marked stale. It also keeps every guide's own links, taken out of that same text at launch, and works out from them which guides point at which — the double-bracket form turned into the ordinary one first, and each link answered by the same following that answers a press. Also renames, moves and throws away a guide — mending the links and the index beside it — and hands out the rows the hierarchy worked out, working them out again whenever any filter moves.
- [managers/Hierarchy.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Hierarchy.ts) — the folders, the files, and the tags on them, made fresh each launch out of the addresses. Holds the walk that turns the folder shape into a list with a depth on every row, and the narrowing: which rows survive the filters, which folders are kept because something inside them matched, how many matching files sit under each folder, and the sort. It also answers a link written inside a guide, by climbing the folders above that guide and taking the first guide of that name found beneath any of them. And, for a report alone, says which file a link that nothing answers most likely meant — never for a press, which must be exact. **⟵ji**, with the storage left behind.
- [managers/Filters.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Filters.ts) — all the filters in one place, so the hierarchy can read them without reaching into anything that draws: the project, the kind, the tags and which way they pick, the words, which folders are shut, whether folders show at all, and which columns sort and which way. Also the three plain answers behind them — whether a file survives the picked tags, what turning them over leaves picked, and which remembered words are still among the choices — each written over plain lists so it can be proved without a page.
- [managers/Operations.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Operations.ts) — which of the two things the content box is doing, which guide is being read, and the stepping between guides. Off the list it walks the stack of guides reached by links — one down, one up, emptied by backing out of the bottom; on the list it walks the filtered run, past folders and wrapping at both ends. Each walk is two halves — one says which file lies a step that way, the other goes there and asks the first — so what a stepper says it would open and what pressing it opens are one answer. It also keeps, for each guide, the line that stood at the top of its words when it was last left, so coming back to one comes back to where the reading was — held with the settings, so the guide being read survives a reload scrolled. **⟵ji**, trimmed from seven modes to two.
- [managers/Status.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Status.ts) — whether there is anything to say along the bottom, and what. One call says a line and starts the few seconds after which it clears itself. What a report found is remembered along with those words, so a reload comes back to the whole report rather than its first line with nothing under it.
- [managers/Preferences.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/managers/Preferences.ts) — what the browser remembers between visits. Every name reads `ov_` then the parts joined by underscores, and the name in the code is the name in the browser. None of ji's renaming and sweeping code came over: overview has no old names to bring forward.
- [database/Indexes.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/database/Indexes.ts) — the instant lookups (tags by file, files by tag, children by folder, parents by file), rebuilt whenever the records change. **⟵ji**
- [types/File.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/types/File.ts) — what a file is: the five kinds, the closed list of thirty-five tags, the five collections, the five labels off a file's top, and a listed row — a file together with the tags on it, how deep it sits, the folder chain above it, and whether it holds anything. The record is called a file rather than a guide, since the list holds work notes too.
- [types/Tag_Areas.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/types/Tag_Areas.ts) — the tags gathered into nine areas, so thirty-five words can be read a handful at a time. Six gather by what a guide is about; `progress` gathers by where a guide stands in its own life — put forward, finished, written up — and `active` by how soon it is wanted: now, next, soon, later, or set aside. The areas are only a way of reading the list, not a second thing to filter by: every tag belongs to exactly one, and the tests prove the two lists agree. Also answers which of an area's tags are still worth showing, and what a shut area reads.
- [types/App.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/types/App.ts) — the two states the app can be in: setting up, and ready.
- [types/DB_Records.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/types/DB_Records.ts) — the record shapes the hierarchy keeps: tags, tag placements, folder links, and link meanings. **⟵ji**
- [utilities/Markdown_Blocks.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Markdown_Blocks.ts) — turning a guide's text into the page on screen, and putting a change back into that text. Takes the five labels off the top; gives every outermost piece two numbers counted from zero against the whole file — the line it starts on and one past the line it ends on — plus the line it begins on counted from one for showing — the whole file, the labels among the lines, so it reads the same as Obsidian's own number for that line — and how deep it sits. Names the headings, gives every link the words its hover says — the name of the file it goes to, without the folders above it or its ending — draws a list item's brackets as a box and turns one over on a press, and draws a rule as an ordinary row so it can carry a number. Says every link a guide's words hold as two things — where it points, and the words it reads as — since only the words are ever drawn. Also hands one piece's own words back out of the file, swaps a run of lines for what was typed leaving every other line untouched, and answers whether those lines still read as they did when the piece was opened. A list item is told the one line it begins on; everything else sitting inside another piece is left unmarked. Also says what markdown stands at the head of a line that the drawn page gives no width to — a heading's hashes, a thing to be done's bullet and brackets — so the box that opens over that line can stand them out to the left.
- [utilities/Index_Files.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Index_Files.ts) — mending the two index files a move leaves lying: reading which file a bulleted link names, taking that line out of one index, and putting it into another in its alphabetical place. An index that lists files in more than one place gets a "More" heading, since nothing in it says which list the arrival belongs to.
- [utilities/Emphasis.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Emphasis.ts) — making a run of picked words heavy, slanted or struck through while a piece is open, and taking the marks off again. Stars stack — one is slanted, two are heavy, three are both — so slanting heavy words adds to them rather than half-undoing them. Also the marks that come in pairs: typing a bracket, brace or quote with words picked puts it before them and its partner after, and typing it again takes both off.
- [utilities/Searching.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Searching.ts) — which piece a search has to open and which it can fold away again, worked out together from the piece open now and the piece the next place needs. Done in the wrong order it folded a piece and opened the same one again on every keystroke, which is what blinked.
- [utilities/Labels.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Labels.ts) — the five labels, read off a file's text and written back into it. Both directions live here, since both are plain work on text and neither needs anything on screen. Reading takes the tags in either shape one can be written in: all on one line, which is what this app writes, or one name to a line, which is what Obsidian rewrites a file into the moment its tags are touched there. A name not on the closed list is dropped and said so. Writing builds the block and swaps it into the file with every word below left alone; a file carrying no labels gets one put at the very top.
- [utilities/Saving.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Saving.ts) — writing a changed file back to its own place. Works out where a guide, a design or a work note sits counting from the top of the repo, and the other way round — a work note counting at the top of its work folder and inside the five folders named here, `next`, `milestones`, `now`, `done` and `proposals`; where a file lands when it is dropped into a folder or given a different name; and hands the dispatcher the whole new text along with the text as it was when the file was opened — which that server checks against the file before writing anything. Also says whether a link reaches deeper into a work folder than that folder's own top, which is the one line saying what the app lists. And asks the dispatcher to start itself over, waiting until it answers again.
- [utilities/Tag_Rows.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Tag_Rows.ts) — whether any tag area in the topmost line of a run shows its own name riding above its top edge. That name reaches up out of the pill, so the run holds a gap above itself to keep it off whatever runs overhead — and only a name in the top line reaches anything. The pills wrap, so which line each is on is measured; the reckoning is written over plain numbers so it can be proved without a page.
- [utilities/Following_Links.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Following_Links.ts) — the two questions a link raises, kept apart on purpose. Whether the file found really answers it: a link naming only a word takes any file of that name, one naming folders must land under exactly those. And, where nothing answers exactly, which file it most likely meant — most shared words first, ties broken by the fewest folder steps from the file the link sits in, and nothing at all offered where two are equal on both. The first answers a press and refuses; the second only ever names a candidate in a report.
- [utilities/Opening_Code.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/utilities/Opening_Code.ts) — handing a piece of the code to VSCode. A guide often names the very file it describes, and those files are not guides, so nothing in the collection answers for them. This says which links name code, works out where that file sits by climbing out of the folder of the guide naming it, and reads the line off the end of the link.

## src/lib/ts/tests/ — the tests

- [markdown_blocks.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/markdown_blocks.test.ts) — the line numbers each piece of a drawn guide claims, taking the labels off the top, putting typed words back in place of a run of lines, drawing a whole guide again after a change, and which markdown at the head of a line the drawn page gives no width to.

- [labels.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/labels.test.ts) — writing the five labels back to the top of a file.
- [index_files.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/index_files.test.ts) — mending the index files a move leaves lying.
- [following_links.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/following_links.test.ts) — whether the file found really answers a link, how far apart two files stand, how many words a link and a place share, and which file a dead link most likely meant.
- [opening_code.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/opening_code.test.ts) — which links name a file of code, where that file sits, and which line the link asks for.
- [tag_rows.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/tag_rows.test.ts) — whether a name rides above a pill in the topmost line of a run of tag areas.
- [tag_areas.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/tag_areas.test.ts) — that the eight areas and the closed tag list agree exactly, what an area offers, and what a shut one reads.
- [core_alias.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/core_alias.test.ts) — that exactly two files reach through the "core" alias: common/Core.ts for everything with exports, and main.ts for the stylesheet alone. A third door fails it.
- [filters.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/filters.test.ts) — which way the picked tags narrow, what turning them over leaves picked, and letting go of a remembered word that is no longer one of the choices.
- [code_blocks.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/code_blocks.test.ts) — a chunk of code counting as one piece: its lines carried on the box and never on the words inside, the language the writer named kept, the fence lines claimed along with the code between them, and a plain paragraph left exactly as it was.
- [emphasis.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/emphasis.test.ts) — heavy, slanted and struck-through words going on and coming off, and the marks that come in pairs: which closes which, putting both either side of what is picked, taking them off from inside or just outside the picking, and typing one with nothing picked. Also that stars stack — slanting heavy words adds to them rather than shaving one off each side — and that a strike and a heaviness are not each other's marks.
- [searching.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/searching.test.ts) — which piece a search opens and which it folds, worked out from the piece open now and the piece the next place needs: nothing when the next place is already inside the open one, and nothing when none was open and none is wanted.
- [wiki_links.test.ts](../../../src/lib/ts/tests/wiki_links.test.ts) — turning double brackets into ordinary links: the guide named and its file pointed at, a name with spaces kept whole, a heading carried through, the words after a bar shown while the name before it is pointed at. Ordinary links, chunks of code, words between single backticks and an empty pair are all left alone, and the count of lines never changes.
